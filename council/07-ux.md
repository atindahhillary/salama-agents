# Council: UX Researcher and Accessibility Lead

## MUST
1. Keep the report instant and ungated, and move the contact step onto it as an optional card under the 30-day plan. A separate step 7 reads as a gate, even when it is labelled optional.
2. No running score while people answer. A visible number tempts people to polish their answers, which corrupts the demand data. Show progress only.
3. Every data and compliance question gets a "Not sure" option, and budget gets "Prefer not to say". Score "Not sure" as a gap and label it "worth checking".
4. Ask about behaviour, not policy: "In the last 3 months, has anyone pasted any of these into ChatGPT or similar?" Make account access its own question, "How do staff log in to email?" (own login plus phone code / own login / shared logins / not sure).
5. Use conditional logic. Ask about parental consent only if the organisation serves children or youth. Name the regulator for the chosen country (each name to be checked by the lawyer).
6. Use native inputs:
   - a fieldset and legend for each question
   - chips built as styled radio buttons or checkboxes
   - 48px touch targets (the WCAG 2.5.8 minimum is 24px)
   - an explicit Next button, with no auto-advance
   - no sliders (2.5.7)
7. Focus: when the step changes, move focus to the step heading and update the page title ("Step 4 of 6: Data and protection"). The sticky Next bar must never cover the field that has focus (2.4.11).
8. Errors: show an error summary at the top and move focus to it. Link inline error text with aria-describedby, and never rely on colour alone. "Pick up to 3" questions show "2 of 3 chosen" and announce the limit in a polite live region.
9. Score:
   - Move focus to the report h1.
   - State the score in plain text: "Score 38 out of 100. Band: Foundations first."
   - Every bar shows its value as text.
   - Bands show as a name plus an icon.
   - Contrast: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for bars.
   - No count-up animation, and honour prefers-reduced-motion.
10. Language: set lang="sw" when the toggle is used. One Kenyan and one Tanzanian native speaker review the Kiswahili.
11. Shared devices:
    - "Clear my answers from this phone" sits in the same place on every step and on the report.
    - After submit, ask "Is this a shared phone?" and offer a one-tap clear.
    - Never autosave contact fields.
    - Drafts expire after 7 days.
12. Page weight: keep the check route under 150 KB compressed. Use system fonts, or one subset woff2 with font-display: swap. No video. Measure on a real mid-range Android over throttled 3G before claiming the page is light on data.

## SHOULD
- **Offline.** Cache the app shell so the check and the rule-based report work with no signal: "Saved on this phone. We will send it when you are back online." Tell people the AI brief needs a connection.
- **Resume and ranges.** Show a resume banner ("Continue where you left off?" / "Start fresh"). Use range chips for hours and budget. Write plain English at about B1 level.
- **Consistent help (3.2.6).** Put "Stuck? WhatsApp us" in the same place on every step.
- **Showing 38/100 without shaming:**
  - Rename "Exposed" to "Foundations first".
  - Open with "What you already do well", then 3 fixes, free ones first, each showing the points it adds (example: "Phone codes on email: +6").
  - Keep exposure as a separate, factual line: "Higher stakes: you serve children."
  - Use amber, never red. No legal threats.
- **Share control.** Offer "Share plan only" or "Plan and score". People forward what will not embarrass them in front of their director.
- **No benchmarks** until real data exists.

## COULD
- A hard-to-guess report link so people can come back later.
- Compare what the redactor catches in free text against "nothing sensitive" answers, as a check on stated versus actual behaviour.

## Redaction demo
Non-technical staff will not understand "tokens" on their own.
- Prefill a made-up field note, give one "Hide personal details" button, and stack before and after.
- Never invite people to paste real notes.
- Be honest about the limits: "It can miss some names."

Framing sentence: "Before any AI reads your notes, we swap names, phone numbers and schools for labels like [PERSON_1], so it can help without knowing who the child is, and it all happens on your phone."

## Flow and microcopy
People are most likely to drop off at:
- Data and protection, where they have to admit gaps
- a long Step 1 that gives nothing back
- the free-text question
- the budget question

So cut Step 1 to type, country and who you serve. The order runs from easy to motivating, then the hard step, then easy again:
1. **About you:** "A few basics so your results fit your organisation."
2. **Your work:** "Which tasks would you most like help with?"
3. **AI today:** "No wrong answers. Starting from zero is fine."
4. **Data and protection:** "The hardest step. 'Not sure' is a useful answer."
5. **Field realities:** "How your teams and communities really connect."
6. **Your plans:** "Last step. This helps us suggest a realistic first move."
7. **Report:** instant, with the optional contact card.

## Test protocol

### Pilot runs
Run 2 internal rehearsals of the full session, one in Kiswahili.

### Participants: 8 people
This early, exploratory test will find the big problems but cannot show differences between groups.
- **Roles:**
  - 3 NGO M&E or programme officers
  - 2 CBO leaders
  - 2 school heads or county officers
  - 1 INGO or funder
- **Within that mix:**
  - at least 3 Kiswahili-first, including 1 Tanzanian
  - at least 5 on their own Android
  - 2 on shared phones
  - 2 remote on mobile data
  - ideally 1 TalkBack user
- **Logistics:**
  - 35 minutes each: 5 in person in Nairobi, 3 on WhatsApp video
  - consent read aloud in their language
  - participants recorded as P1 to P8 only

### Tasks
1. "What does this site offer, and what does the box on this page do?"
2. "Your director asked if your organisation is ready to use AI safely. Find out here." At step 3, ask them to lock the phone and pick it up again 2 minutes later.
3. "Tell me what this result means and what you would do first."
4. "Send this to whoever would decide on it."
5. "This office phone is shared. Make sure the next person cannot see your answers."

### Think-aloud prompts
1. "What are you thinking right now?"
2. "What do you expect if you tap that?"
3. "You paused. What was going through your mind?"
4. "How would you ask this to a colleague?"
5. "Is anything here uncomfortable to answer? Why?"

### What to measure
- Completion per task: unaided, assisted or failed.
- Time per step and in total. Is "7 minutes" true?
- The step where people drop off.
- Confusion per question: a pause over 10 seconds, tapping back, misreading, or asking for help.
- How often each question gets "Not sure".
- Whether participants can explain the score and the demo in their own words.
- Usefulness rating from 1 to 5.
- Real forwards within 48 hours, tracked with a separate ?ref= code per participant.

Report results as counts ("6 of 8"), not percentages. Keep what people did separate from what you think it means, and rate each finding critical, major or minor.

### Recruiting
Send one-to-one WhatsApp messages to contacts from your live deployments, and ask two trusted CBO leaders to forward it. Do not post in large groups. Screen by reply: role, organisation type, country, phone, language. As thanks, give a data bundle plus their tailored brief.

> "Habari [name]. I am testing a free tool that helps NGOs and schools check if they are ready to use AI safely. Could you try it on your phone for 35 minutes? You get a data bundle and your own report. Reply NDIO for 4 quick questions."
